History Of Channa Masala

Chickpeas were cultivated for thousands of years in the Middle East and India. The Portuguese introduction of tomatoes and Central Asian introduction of onions, garlic, ginger later added the essential heat and flavor to the chickpea curry by the 19th century. During the Mughal Era (16th-19th century), the influence of Persian culture introduced aromatic spices (coriander, cumin, turmeric, garam masala) to India, becoming the basis of what makes a good Channa Masala.

  • Drain the chickpeas, but save the water
  • Dice the tomatoes and onions
  • Set aside the spices

Steps

  • Put a kadhai (a bowl-shaped frying pan) or pan on medium heat
  • Add the oil and cumin seeds once it heats up
  • Add the garam masala and sauté
  • Once fully combined, add the onions and ginger garlic paste; sauté until softened and caramelized
  • Add the tomatoes and slowly sprinkle in the turmeric, chili powder, coriander powder, and cumin powder
  • As it softens, add the water and soaked + cooked chickpeas and let it simmer
  • Finally, add the salt to your liking and garnish with the coriander

    • Tips

  • Replace chili powder with Kashmiri chili powder
  • Garnish with fresh lemon and onions for tang
  • Eat it with rice or naan
  • Have a side of curd (yogurt) alongsid
